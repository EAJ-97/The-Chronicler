The caravan reached Saltmere as the tide drew back, and the city rose from the flats like something the sea had forgotten to take. Gulls wheeled over leaning towers of whitewashed stone. Somewhere below, bells answered one another across the canals.

Brannoc found them rooms at the Lantern House, an old customs hall turned inn, where every window held a candle that was never allowed to go out. That night, sleep came hard. Wren dreamed of the burning orchard again, the smoke thick enough to taste. Brannoc slept like a stone and woke hungry.

Morning brought word from the harbor: the Tidewardens had closed the outer gate, and no one would say why.

The old woman at the gate did not look up when they approached. She only spoke.

> "You carry a debt you have not yet been asked to pay. When the asking comes, do not refuse it."

Wren said nothing for a long while after that.
